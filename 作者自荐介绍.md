《AI赋能的生态统计》是系统阐述AI时代生态统计方法体系的专著，旨在为生态学及相关学科读者提供运用R语言进行生态数据分析的系统参考。全书共八章，围绕天童森林动态监测样地这一贯穿案例，系统讲解从AI辅助编程、概率分布到假设检验、回归建模与模型选择的生态统计方法。示例来自真实生态学研究，图表由R代码生成，源代码随书公开。

本书填补了国内生态统计专著AI融合的空白。传统教材多将统计方法与工具割裂讲授，很少涉及AI时代可解释性与预测精度权衡、虚假相关与因果混淆、空间自相关数据泄露等方法论挑战。本书将经典统计与AI思维贯穿同一框架，追问"为什么这样做"与"局限性是什么"。从学科发展看，本书推动了生态统计学向AI融合范式转型；从科学研究看，可重复研究理念有助于提升生态研究公信力；从国民经济看，碳储量评估、生物多样性核算等方法可支撑碳交易定价与生态补偿政策。本书围绕分解复杂生态问题、与AI协作、对模型输出独立判断三项核心分析能力展开论述。

本书是作者多年教学实践基础上首次系统撰写的完整专著。国内现有生态统计教材鲜有涉及AI方法论挑战，国际同类著作对国内读者可及性不足。本书创新体现在：学术思想上，提出"AI协作而不盲从"，将批判性思维融入各章方法论述；内容范围上，涵盖频率学派、贝叶斯估计、模拟检验与机器学习，探讨因果推断等前沿话题；结构体系上，以同一案例贯穿全书，采用问题导向论述，让读者经历完整科研分析过程；写作特点上，以隐喻降低认知门槛，各章通过概要性论述阐明方法的理论定位与应用场景。

本书读者包括生态学、环境科学、生物学等专业的本科生和硕士研究生，以及从事生态保护的科研和管理人员。在"双碳"和生态文明建设背景下，自然保护区规划、生态红线划定、环境影响评估等领域对数据分析人才需求持续增长，急需兼具生态知识与数据技能的复合型人才。具备扎实统计与AI协作能力的读者在学术深造和职业发展中具有显著竞争优势。

全书以R语言为主要工具。第一章奠定AI辅助编程基础；第二章讲解概率论与生态分布模型；第四章阐述参数估计与物种丰富度推断；第五章介绍相关性分析；第六章讲解假设检验与多重比较；第七章介绍模拟检验范式；第八章建立回归建模框架；第九章聚焦模型选择与验证。全书统计思维与AI批判性协作素养并重，构建从"会用工具"到"理解方法"再到"独立思考"的方法论进阶。

Ecological Statistics with AI is an academic monograph on ecological data analysis in the AI era, offering ecology and related discipline readers a systematic reference for data analysis with R. Organized around the Tiantong Mountain forest dynamics plot as a unifying case study, the book systematically covers core ecological statistical methods across eight chapters, from AI-assisted programming, probability distributions, and parameter estimation through hypothesis testing, regression modeling, and model selection. All examples are drawn from real ecological studies; nearly every figure is produced by R code, and the full source code is publicly available.

This book addresses a notable gap: existing ecological statistics texts rarely integrate AI. Traditional works tend to treat statistical methods and computational tools separately, seldom engaging with the methodological challenges that the AI era brings—tensions between interpretability and predictive accuracy, spurious correlations mistaken for causation, and data leakage due to spatial autocorrelation. By weaving classical statistics and AI thinking into a single framework, the book persistently asks why a method is chosen and what its limitations are. It contributes to the paradigm shift of ecological statistics toward AI integration. Its emphasis on reproducible research strengthens the credibility of ecological studies. And at the practical level, methods such as carbon stock assessment and biodiversity accounting can directly support carbon trading markets and ecological compensation policy. Grounded in a problem-driven approach, the book develops three core analytical competencies: breaking down complex ecological problems, collaborating effectively with AI, and exercising independent judgment over model outputs.

The book is the author's first comprehensive monograph, developed through years of teaching and research at East China Normal University. Compared to existing works, Chinese ecological statistics books rarely address AI-related methodological issues, while international ones often assume a graduate-level background and remain less accessible to domestic readers. The book's innovations can be summarized along four dimensions. In academic philosophy, it champions collaborating with AI rather than deferring to it, integrating critical thinking into the methodological discussion of each chapter. In scope, it is the first single volume to span frequentist methods, Bayesian estimation, simulation-based tests, and machine learning, while engaging with frontier topics such as causal inference and conformal prediction. In structure, a single case study runs through every chapter in a problem-driven narrative, so that readers experience the full arc of research: encountering a problem, recognizing the need for a method, understanding the principles, and applying them to reach a conclusion. In writing style, the book uses metaphor to make abstract concepts accessible, and each chapter opens with an integrated overview that connects theoretical foundations with practical applications.

The intended audience includes undergraduate and graduate students in ecology, environmental science, and biology, as well as researchers and practitioners working in ecological conservation. With China's dual carbon targets and broader ecological civilization agenda, fields such as nature reserve planning, ecological redline delineation, and environmental impact assessment face a rapidly growing demand for professionals who combine ecological expertise with data skills. Readers who develop a solid foundation in statistics and the ability to work alongside AI will carry a distinct advantage into graduate study and the job market.

Using R as its primary tool, the book is organized as follows. Chapter 1 lays the groundwork for AI-assisted R programming. Chapter 2 covers probability theory and ecological distribution models. Chapter 4 addresses parameter estimation, including maximum likelihood, Bayesian estimation, and species richness inference. Chapter 5 introduces correlation analysis. Chapter 6 treats hypothesis testing and multiple comparisons. Chapter 7 presents simulation-based testing paradigms. Chapter 8 builds the regression modeling framework. Chapter 9 focuses on model selection and validation. Throughout, the book gives equal weight to statistical thinking and critical AI collaboration skills, charting a methodological progression from using the tools, through understanding the methods, to thinking independently.
