本书是作者多年教学实践基础上首次系统编写的完整教程。国内现有生态统计教材鲜有涉及AI方法论挑战，国际教材对本科生可及性不足。本书创新体现在：学术思想上，提出"AI协作而不盲从"，将批判性思维转化为各章AI素养目标；内容范围上，涵盖频率学派、贝叶斯估计、模拟检验与机器学习，探讨因果推断等前沿话题；结构体系上，以同一案例贯穿全书，采用"学徒式"叙事，让读者经历完整科研认知过程；写作特点上，以隐喻降低认知门槛，每章设知识、技能、AI素养三层目标。

Ecological Statistics with AI is a textbook for undergraduate and graduate ecology students that builds competence in ecological data analysis with R while working alongside AI tools. Organized around a single case study—the Tiantong Mountain forest dynamics plot—the book follows Chang Lin, a master's student, through her research journey. Across eight chapters, it systematically covers core ecological statistical methods, from AI-assisted programming, probability distributions, and parameter estimation through hypothesis testing, regression modeling, and model selection. All examples are drawn from real ecological studies; nearly every figure is produced by R code, and the full source code is publicly available.

This book addresses a notable gap: existing ecological statistics textbooks rarely integrate AI. Traditional texts tend to treat statistical methods and computational tools separately, seldom engaging with the methodological challenges that the AI era brings—tensions between interpretability and predictive accuracy, spurious correlations mistaken for causation, and data leakage due to spatial autocorrelation. By weaving classical statistics and AI thinking into a single framework, the book persistently asks why a method is chosen and what its limitations are. It contributes to the paradigm shift of ecological statistics toward AI integration. Its emphasis on reproducible research strengthens the credibility of ecological studies. And at the practical level, methods such as carbon stock assessment and biodiversity accounting can directly support carbon trading markets and ecological compensation policy. Grounded in a learning-by-doing approach, the book cultivates three core competencies: breaking down complex ecological problems, collaborating effectively with AI, and exercising independent judgment over model outputs.

The book is the author's first comprehensive textbook, developed through years of teaching at East China Normal University. Compared to existing texts, Chinese ecological statistics books rarely address AI-related methodological issues, while international ones often assume a graduate-level background and remain less accessible to undergraduates. The book's innovations can be summarized along four dimensions. In academic philosophy, it champions collaborating with AI rather than deferring to it, translating critical thinking into chapter-level AI literacy goals. In scope, it is the first single volume to span frequentist methods, Bayesian estimation, simulation-based tests, and machine learning, while engaging with frontier topics such as causal inference and conformal prediction. In structure, a single case study runs through every chapter in an apprenticeship-style narrative, so that readers experience the full arc of research: encountering a problem, recognizing the need for a method, understanding the principles, and applying them to reach a conclusion. In writing style, the book uses metaphor to make abstract concepts accessible, and each chapter is built around three tiers of learning objectives: knowledge, skills, and AI literacy.

The intended audience includes undergraduate and graduate students in ecology, environmental science, and biology, as well as researchers and practitioners working in ecological conservation. With China's dual carbon targets and broader ecological civilization agenda, fields such as nature reserve planning, ecological redline delineation, and environmental impact assessment face a rapidly growing demand for professionals who combine ecological expertise with data skills. Students who develop a solid foundation in statistics and the ability to work alongside AI will carry a distinct advantage into graduate study and the job market.

Using R as its primary tool, the book is organized as follows. Chapter 1 lays the groundwork for AI-assisted R programming. Chapter 2 covers probability theory and ecological distribution models. Chapter 4 addresses parameter estimation, including maximum likelihood, Bayesian estimation, and species richness inference. Chapter 5 introduces correlation analysis. Chapter 6 treats hypothesis testing and multiple comparisons. Chapter 7 presents simulation-based testing paradigms. Chapter 8 builds the regression modeling framework. Chapter 9 focuses on model selection and validation. Throughout, the book gives equal weight to cultivating statistical thinking and critical AI collaboration skills, charting a path from knowing how to use the tools, through understanding the methods, to thinking independently.
